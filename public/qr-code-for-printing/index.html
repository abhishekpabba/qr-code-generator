QR codes for printing

For print work, the QR code should stay sharp at the final physical size and leave enough contrast and clear space for a phone camera to detect it quickly. The download resolution and file format matter more than making the on-screen preview look larger.

Choose the right export

SVGBest choice for scalable print artwork because it stays sharp at different sizes.
PDFConvenient for print workflows and sending finished artwork to a printer.
PNGGood when you need a raster image. Use a high enough pixel resolution for the final size.
JPGUseful for general digital output, but PNG/SVG/PDF are usually better choices for QR printing.

Common print applications

  • Business cards
  • Product labels and stickers
  • Flyers and postcards
  • Restaurant table signs
  • Packaging and inserts
  • Event signage

Scan reliability checklist

  • Use a dark QR pattern on a light background whenever possible.
  • Keep the quiet zone around the code clear.
  • Do not make a center logo so large that it removes too much QR data.
  • Use higher error correction when adding a logo.
  • Print one sample at the final physical size and test it before a large production run.

Resolution is for export, not preview

A 4096px QR code should not look physically larger in the live preview than a 512px QR code. Higher resolution simply gives the downloaded image more pixels for printing. The final printed dimensions are controlled later in your design or print layout.
