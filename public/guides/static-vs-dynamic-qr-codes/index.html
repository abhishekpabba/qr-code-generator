Static vs dynamic QR codes

Both types look like ordinary QR codes, but the destination works differently. A static QR code stores the final information directly in the code. A dynamic QR code usually stores a short redirect URL that sends the scanner to the current destination.

Static QR codes

Static codes are ideal when the encoded information is unlikely to change. Common examples include WiFi credentials, a permanent website URL, contact information or plain text.

  • No redirect service is required.
  • The code keeps working as long as the encoded destination itself remains valid.
  • You cannot change the encoded destination after printing without creating a new QR code.

Dynamic QR codes

Dynamic codes are useful when you may need to change the destination later or measure scans. The printed QR code stays the same while the redirect destination can be updated behind the scenes.

  • Edit the destination after the code has been printed.
  • Measure scan counts when analytics are enabled.
  • Useful for campaigns, menus, events and packaging where links may change.

Which should you use?

Use caseRecommended
Permanent websiteStatic
WiFi credentialsStatic
Restaurant menu that may changeDynamic
Marketing campaign with scan analyticsDynamic
Business card contact detailsStatic

One important consideration

A dynamic QR code depends on the redirect service continuing to work. For long-term printed materials, use a service you control or trust. Static QR codes do not have that dependency because the final content is encoded directly in the QR pattern.
